**What it was**: `go vet` and the .NET analysers were detected and not measured: they were the last two systems with a "no reader yet", and with them the last two languages without lint on the map.

**What changes**: **go vet** runs as `go vet ./...` with the `go vet` on the PATH and its lines are read —`./pkg/a.go:12:3: message`—; `# package` headers and the command's own errors are not findings. Since its text does not sign the analyser, its messages enter with the `go vet` rule instead of inventing a name. **.NET** runs with `dotnet format <solution|project> analyzers --verify-no-changes --no-restore --severity info` —the solution wins over a single project— and the MSBuild lines it writes are read: `path(12,5): warning CA1822: …`, with the diagnostic code as the rule, its severity and its position; the trailing `[project]` is not part of the message. Both write to stderr and the command merges it with `2>&1`. The override gains the `govet-text` and `dotnet-text` dialects.

**Why this way**: neither gives a JSON worth having without a build or without depending on a version, and their text is the output they have been giving for years. Rules are not enumerated for either: `go vet` analysers go with the Go version and .NET ones with the SDK and `AnalysisLevel`, so the note says so and no ageing list is filled in.

**Verified**: tests for both readers —headers and foreign lines left out, relative paths, severities and the `[project]` stripped—, for detection with `go.mod`, with a solution and with a single project, and for dispatching in `runLint`. A real .NET run on a temporary console: CA1822 entered with its line and column. The Go one used a stub binary, because Go is not installed here. Full suite (1,273), lint, typecheck and site build green.

**What is missing**: the active rules for `go vet` and .NET —not enumerable without their version's catalogue— and lint in the MCP.

**Verdict**: phase in progress.
